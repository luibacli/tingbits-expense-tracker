# SaaS analysis: Tingbits Expense Tracker

- Date: 2026-11-10
- Stage: EXCOM Gate 1 (discovery) and Gate 2 (analysis, reference audit). Nothing in this document is built yet.
- Owner's brief: "help everyone, families, track their finances", make it a SaaS, add a landing page, **₱150 per month**.
- Prices are dated and sourced (see Sources). USD→PHP uses **₱58 per $1**, an assumption to re-check before publishing anything that compares prices.

## 0. The name: Tingbits (2026-11-10)
**Tingbits** (also *tingbitay*, from *ting bitay*) is Cebuano/Bisaya slang for **the broke days before payday**, when a family stretches what's left until the next sweldo. Usage: *"Tingbits napud"*, roughly "broke again" [8][9][10].

Why it works:
- **It names the exact problem the product ends.** The promise "make it to payday" and the brand are the same idea, so the name explains the app.
- **Ownable and memorable:** a real local word with a story, in a category of generic names ("Money Manager", "Expense Tracker", "Wallet").
- **Native to the audience:** Cebuano speakers recognise it immediately. It also gives the product a Visayas/Mindanao identity next to Tagalog phrases like "Saan napunta ang sweldo?".

Risks and how the page handles them:
- **Not everyone knows the word**, for example Tagalog speakers. The landing page defines it before the headline, like a listahan entry ("ting·bits (Bisaya) · the broke days before payday"), and answers "What does tingbits mean?" in the FAQ.
- **It means being broke.** Always frame the app as the way *out* ("Goodbye, tingbits."), never as an app for broke people.
- **Etymology:** the sources agree on the meaning but only one breaks down the origin (*ting* + *bitay*). Don't make claims about where the word comes from.
- **Not checked yet:** trademark (IPOPHL), domain names and social handles. Check before printing or buying anything.
- **Bisaya phrasing:** only "Tingbits napud" (sourced) is used. Have a native speaker review any new Bisaya copy.

Naming in use: **Tingbits Expense Tracker** (full name: page titles, sign-in, search). **Tingbits** (short: app header, home-screen icon). Plan name: **Tingbits Family**.

## 1. The problem we solve
Most Filipino families don't lack a budget; they lack **a number they can act on today**.

- **The money arrives twice a month** (kinsenas and katapusan). The days just before payday are where it runs out.
- **Spending is spread across people and pockets:** cash, GCash/Maya, a card, a partner's wallet. Nobody sees the whole picture until it's gone, and "Saan napunta ang sweldo?" has no answer.
- **Bills sneak up:** Meralco, water, internet, rent, tuition, the paluwagan contribution.
- **Many families still keep a *listahan*** (a notebook of expenses) or split pay into ***sobre*** (envelopes). These work, but nobody can see them from work or the jeepney.

BSP's 2025 Consumer Finance and Inclusion Survey: 74% of adults pass the basic financial literacy test, and most keep personal budgets, yet only **43% are satisfied with their financial condition** [1]. People know *how* to budget. What's missing is a daily tool that the whole household shares.

## 2. Who it is for
| Segment | Situation | What they need from us |
|---|---|---|
| **Family money manager** (primary): often the mother or the partner who "holds the wallet" | Runs the household budget, keeps a listahan, pays the bills | One daily number; everyone's spending in one place; bills on time |
| **Couples** | Two incomes, shared bills, different spending styles | Shared ledger, "Added by", no arguments about where it went |
| **Families receiving remittances** | Money arrives from an OFW and has to last | Make a fixed amount last until the next padala; show the sender where it went (export) |
| **Young earners** living paycheck to paycheck | First job, first payday cycle | Learn to pace spending to payday |

These segments are **hypotheses** from the brief and the BSP data. Check them with 5 to 10 real households before spending on ads.

## 3. Reference audit (6 sites, screenshots taken 2026-11-10)
| Site | What it does well | Borrow | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| YNAB | Leads with a feeling ("Do you worry about money?"), clear trial, "no credit card required" | Emotional opening; trial with no card | Flying-money illustration; US-only bank framing |
| Monarch | Calm, premium tone; couples have their own page | Calm confidence; a section for households and couples | Laptop dashboard as hero; stock lifestyle photo |
| Goodbudget | Envelope method made digital, "for family and friends" | Familiar household method (our *sobre*) | Clip-art illustration; dense tiny text |
| Honeydue | "Together" as the core promise | Shared-money message | Generic phone mock-ups |
| Money Lover | Known in the Philippines; simple message | Plain language | "1 Million+ users" style claims (we have no such proof); generic headline |
| Spendee | Strong product screenshots | Show the real product | Cookie-consent wall on arrival; pie-chart hero |

**What everyone does:** a phone or laptop dashboard hero, green "money" colors, generic headlines ("The modern way to manage money", "Simple way to manage personal finances"), US bank linking.

**What nobody does:** talk about **payday**, give the family **one number for today**, or charge **one price for the whole household**. That is our space.

## 4. Positioning
> **For Filipino families** who feel the money runs out before payday, **Tingbits Expense Tracker** is the shared family money tracker that tells you **every morning how much you can safely spend today**. Unlike budgeting apps built for one person and US bank accounts, Tingbits is made for the whole household (cash and e-wallets, kinsenas and katapusan) and costs **₱150 a month for everyone**.

## 5. The statement (how it makes their lives better)
**Recommended headline:**
> **Know what your family can spend today, and still make it to payday.**

**Supporting line:**
> Tingbits turns your budget into one number every morning. Everyone in the house logs spending in seconds, even without signal, and the bills never sneak up on you.

**Short tagline:** *Make it to payday, every payday.*
**Filipino line (for social and ads):** *Alam ng buong pamilya kung magkano pa ang pwedeng gastusin ngayong araw.*

Why this statement:
- It promises an **outcome** (making it to payday), not features. Competitors sell "managing money".
- "Today" is concrete and daily, which is how families actually feel money.
- "Your family" says the whole household, and the single household price makes it true.
- It is honest: the app really does all of this today (safe to spend today, shared ledger, offline, recurring bills).

**Five lasting benefits** (each mapped to something already built):
1. **Calm about money every day.** The "safe to spend today" number. *(built)*
2. **Everyone on the same page.** One ledger, invite code, "Added by", who paid. *(built)*
3. **Bills never forgotten.** Recurring bills post themselves or remind you. *(built)*
4. **Works the way you live.** 3-second logging, cash and e-wallets, works offline, payday cycles (15th/30th). *(built)*
5. **See where it went.** Insights, trends and exports for the family meeting or the OFW sender. *(built)*

Claims we must **not** make until they are true: user numbers, testimonials, savings figures ("families save ₱X"), bank-grade or BSP-regulated wording, "GCash autopay".

- **Tingbits Family: ₱150/month.** Everything included, unlimited family members, cancel anytime.

- Optional later: annual **₱1,500** (2 months free). Not shown now, because you asked for the monthly price only.

## 7. What "SaaS" adds to the current app
| # | Item | Why | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Landing page** at `/` (public, prerendered for search), app moves under `/app` | Visitors need to understand and trust before signing up | This analysis + approved creative direction |
| S2 | **Legal pages**: Terms, Privacy Policy, refund policy | Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) and payment-provider requirements | Owner's legal details; review by a lawyer recommended |
| S3 | **Trial and subscription status** per household; read-only mode when lapsed (data never deleted). **Done 2026-11-10 (ADR-008).** | Charging ₱150 | Approved |
| S4 | **Payments** with PayMongo (card + Maya recurring); webhooks; receipts | Getting paid | Owner's **business registration** and PayMongo account (KYC) |
| S5 | **Password reset and email verification** | Paying customers must be able to recover access | Email provider |
| S6 | **Account deletion and data export** for users | Privacy law and trust | |
| S7 | Vercel **Pro**, custom domain, support email | Commercial hosting terms, credibility | Owner's accounts |

Order: **S1 can be built now.** S3 and S4 need your PayMongo and business setup. S2 needs your legal details. Launching paid sign-ups before S2 to S6 exist would be a promise the build can't keep (kit: proposal reconciliation).

## 8. Proposed business rules (need your yes/no)
| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| P1 | One subscription covers one household, with unlimited members. |
| P2 | 30-day free trial on household creation, no card. |
| P3 | When a trial or subscription ends, the household becomes **read-only** (view and export, no new entries) until paid. Data is never deleted for non-payment. |
| P4 | The household **owner** pays and manages billing. |
| P5 | Payment by card or Maya recurring; GCash once PayMongo enables recurring GCash (or as a manual monthly payment). |
| P6 | Existing households (created before billing) get the trial from the billing launch date. |

## 9. Risks
- **Commercial hosting:** must move to Vercel Pro before taking money.
- **Payment setup** needs a registered business and KYC: allow 1 to 3 weeks.
- **GCash autopay** may not be available through PayMongo. GCash is the most expected wallet, so be clear on the pricing page about what is accepted.
- **Trust:** a new brand asking families for money data. Answer it with no bank linking needed, privacy promises we keep, and data export anytime.
- **Search and ads** in the category are crowded. The payday angle and Filipino copy are the edge.
